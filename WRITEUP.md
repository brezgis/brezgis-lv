# Brezgi — a chronicle of one spot of earth

*Companion write-up to the interactive reconstruction of Taurenes pagasts, Vidzeme, Latvia.
Four moments in time on the same real terrain: ~AD 50, ~AD 950, 1860, and 1935.*

---

## 1. The place, and how it was rebuilt

The simulation covers a real 4.8 × 4.8 km square of the Vidzeme Upland centred on
**57.15944 N, 25.66472 E** — the centre of Taurene village, in the old Cēsis district.
The ground you stand on is the actual elevation model of that square [18]; the Gauja's
course, the outline of Taurenes ezers and the little Dzērbe stream are the mapped,
real geometries [19]; Dabaru ezers, which the young Gauja flows through just southwest
of the village, was recovered from the flat lake surface visible in the elevation data
(its true published size is 17.8 ha) [4].

The Gauja here is very young — it has only just left its source basin at Lake Alauksts
and is threading a chain of seven glacial through-flow lakes: Bānūžu, Ilzes, Rijas,
Brenkūžu, Stupēnu, Dabaru, Taurenes [4]. The land is morainic hill country: spruce on
the heights, birch and alder in the damp hollows, fields on the gentle slopes. In
summer heat, the Culture Canon says of these hills, the forests are smoky blue; under
rain clouds, almost black [8].

On the hill west of Dabaru ezers stands **Lejstupu pilskalns** (also Briediņu
pilskalns), a state-protected ancient hillfort — one of thirteen registered
archaeological monuments in this one parish [10]. It is in the simulation, where it
really is.

## 2. The name Brezgis

Three facts, and one honest inference.

**Fact one.** About six kilometres south of the village, on the road that is now the
P30, lies the settlement **Brezģis**, historically also called **Brežģa krogs** — the
Brezga tavern [4]. In the nineteenth century the Nēķena manor brewery sold its ale to
the taverns at *Brezgi* and *Lāči* [2].

**Fact two.** Beside it rises **Brežģa kalns**, at 255.4 m the highest point of the
parish — a kilometre-long forested ridge, also once called Karatavu kalns, "Gallows
Hill," with an oak at its summit. It forces the Gauja into a great loop toward
Taurene. In independent Latvia it was the parish's festival hill: Jāņi bonfires every
midsummer, open-air theatre in 1937–39, until the Soviet army came in 1940 [7][2].

**Fact three.** Vidzeme peasants took hereditary surnames in the soul revision of
1826, and very commonly took them from the farmstead or landmark where they lived
[5][6].

**The inference:** a family named Brezgis almost certainly carries the name of this
place — the tavern, the farm, the hill. That is not documented in a published
genealogy we could find; it is the ordinary, overwhelmingly common pattern of Latvian
surname formation applied to a name that exists exactly once in the landscape, in this
parish. The simulated farmstead is placed near the village core (where the manor,
school and river ensemble can be seen together); the real Brezgi ground lies just
beyond the southern edge of the map, up the river valley. The southern river view
looks toward it.

## 3. ~AD 50 — where the aurochs grazed

The Early Iron Age Vidzeme Upland was near-wilderness. Pollen studies show woodland
persisting around Cēsis right through the Iron Age; settlement evidence in the upland
is thin and comes mostly from graves — collective stone *tarand* graves of Finnic
tradition and sand barrows of the Balts [13][15]. We show a seasonal hunters' camp on
the river terrace: a lean-to, a fish rack, a dugout canoe, a fire.

And the aurochs. *Taurs* is the Latvian word for the wild ox *Bos primigenius*, and
Latvian linguistics counts place-names like Taurene as evidence that aurochs lived in
these forests [16]. Their bones and tooth-pendants are known from Latvian Stone Age
sites; they survived in Lithuania and East Prussia into the 13th–16th centuries, and
the very last one died in Poland in 1627 [17]. At AD 50 they would have been rare in
Latvia — a declining forest population — but their presence on a Vidzeme river meadow
is defensible, and here, of all places, it is the point. The herd in the simulation
grazes the terrace where the farm will one day stand: *kur tauri ganījās*. Bulls are
shown near-black with the pale dorsal "eel stripe," cows red-brown, as the sources
describe the species. Elk — always abundant here — watch from the trees.

## 4. ~AD 950 — a Latgalian farmstead

By the Late Iron Age eastern Vidzeme was Latgalian country [13]. Our farmstead is
built on the best archaeological evidence in the Baltic: **Āraiši lake fortress**,
25 km west, whose timbers were felled in **AD 835** — a date fixed to the single year
by the cosmic-ray spike of AD 774/5 in its tree rings [11]. From Āraiši come the
small log dwellings of about thirty square metres with tongue-and-groove corners and
oak pins, moss and tow in the joints; the clay stove at the centre of the room; the
low gable roofs of birch and spruce bark held down by weight poles (reproduced in the
simulation); the granary raised against damp and mice; the crucibles of a resident
bronze-caster; rye, barley, wheat and turnips; fishing, hunting and forest
beekeeping [11][12].

The animals are deliberately small: Iron Age Baltic cattle stood barely over a metre
at the shoulder, the horses were pony-sized forest animals ancestral to the
Žemaitukas, the sheep dark and primitive. Chickens had only recently arrived in the
eastern Baltic, so the yard has just a few [14]. Across the river, the palisaded
refuge on Lejstupu hill guards the lake shore — hillforts of this kind were the
storm-shelters and chiefly seats of the Latgalian landscape [10][13]. The low, fresh
barrow mounds by the field follow Latgalian practice exactly as the transition to
barrow burial began in the 9th–10th centuries: hemispherical, four to seven metres
across, men laid with heads to the east, women to the west [13]. Under the old oak,
offerings — the Balts worshipped at trees and groves, not in temples.

*A note on honesty:* the barrows are kept in the terrain in all four eras (earth
mounds persist), which slightly haunts the AD 50 scene where they would not yet
exist. Consider them a memory the land is about to acquire.

## 5. 1860 — Brezgi under Nēķens manor

The parish is Nēķene now — *Nötkenshof* — named for the Notken family who took the
estate in 1601; the deed-chain runs back to 1426, when the Riga archbishop granted
"Gerslau" to his vassal [3]. Since 1856 the lords are the **von Panders** [2][3].
Serfdom in Vidzeme ended in 1819 — freedom without land — and the surnames came in
1826; money rent was slowly replacing corvée, and within a decade families like this
one would begin buying their farms outright [5][6].

The farmstead is the classic **viensēta**, verified detail by detail against the
Ethnographic Open-Air Museum's Vidzeme homestead [8]: the dwelling with its first
chimney and small glass panes; the great thatched **rija** (threshing barn — thatch
lasted longest there, preserved by the grain-drying smoke); the shingled **klēts**,
the only locked building on the farm; the long windowless byre; the **pirts** smoking
by the river on Saturday; the well-sweep; the slanted **riķu** fence; log beehives;
hay on pole-stacks; the orchard.

Across the fields, the old classicist manor house watches from its linden park. On
the riverbank below, the manor **brewery** — real, documented — cooled 25,000 buckets
of ale a year in three vaulted stone cellars dug into the Gauja's slope, and sold it,
among other places, at the Brezgi tavern [2]. In 1905, when revolution burned the
manors of Vecpiebalga, Dzērbene and Sērmūkši, Nēķena survived: its own peasants and
servants stood against the arsonists, a fact remembered to the Panders' credit —
though the next year the tsarist field court-martial moved into the manor and made a
prison of its cellars [2][3].

## 6. 1935 — Taurene

Independent Latvia's agrarian reform of 1920 took the manors into the State Land
Fund; Nēķena's 1,150 hectares alone became 72 new farms [9][3]. The German
aristocrats left. And in 1925 the parish shed its German name.

The man who renamed it was **Kārlis Bormanis** (1877–1958) — born on a farmstead in
this parish, teacher at the Taurene primary school, choir director, author of
*"Zeme"*, Latvia's first school geography textbook, holder of the Order of the Three
Stars. At the parish council he argued that the community should not forget its own
history, and proposed restoring the ancient name recorded here in the 1430s:
**Taurene** — from *taurs*, the aurochs. It took effect on 1 January 1926 [1][2][3].
The parish coat of arms bears aurochs horns to this day [2]. The exact phrase "where
the aurochs once grazed" survives as local tradition rather than a documented quote —
Bormanis was a schoolmaster more than a poet — but the poetry came anyway: decades
later Māra Zālīte wrote the lines the parish still quotes, *"Kur tu skriesi, tauriņ
mans? — Uz Taureni, uz Taureni!"* [20]. The memorial stone by the road in the
simulation carries that inheritance.

The scene: the brick **new manor** (1888, architect R. G. Šmēlings, neo-Renaissance
with its Italian villa air) now houses the parish's civic life, the Latvian flag over
the door [2][3]. The farm has a shingle roof, framed glass windows, a porch, an
orchard; telephone poles follow the gravel road; the white stork keeps its wheel-top
nest; the cream goes to the cooperative dairy. And on Jāņi night, fires burn on the
hills — in the simulation on the old fort hill, and in life, most famously, on Brežģa
kalns itself, as they did every midsummer until 1940 [7][2].

## 7. What is real, what is interpretation

**Real, from data:** the terrain (public elevation tiles) [18]; the Gauja's course,
Taurenes ezers, the Dzērbe (OpenStreetMap) [19]; Dabaru ezers (elevation-derived
outline at its true location) [4]; the hillfort location on Dabaru's west shore [10].

**Documented, reconstructed interpretively:** the Āraiši-type buildings and their
roofs [11][12]; the viensēta building set [8]; the manor ensemble, brewery and park
[2][3]; livestock species and sizes [14]; burial customs [13]; the renaming [1][3];
Brežģa kalns and its fires [7].

**Interpretive license, flagged:** the farmstead's placement at the village core
(the real Brezgi ground is ~6 km south, just off the map); the barrow mounds visible
in eras before they were raised; the watermill (typical for a Gauja manor, not
specifically documented at Nēķena); the exact yard layouts; everything about the
weather. No people are depicted — the village keeps their traces instead: smoke,
laundry, a loaded cart, an axe left in the block.

## Second edition (same day): the map grows, and time deepens

The reconstruction now covers **8.8 × 8.8 km**, so the real Brezgi ground is on the
map: the Brezģis settlement / Brežģa krogs site and **Brežģa kalns** itself, with the
old Cēsis–Vecpiebalga road climbing over the hill's flank, the Jāņi pyre on the summit
(fires attested there through the 1930s [7]), the summit oak, and — in 2025 — the
eleven-metre observation tower of 2017 [24]. The manor's ale, documented as sold at the
Brezgi tavern, now has its tavern [2].

Two eras were added at the ends of time. **~10,800 BC**: Younger Dryas tundra —
dwarf-birch heath, glacial erratics, reindeer, a milky meltwater Gauja, dead ice
melting in the future lake basins (kettle-hole collapse continued into the early
Holocene [21]); no humans yet — Latvia's first reindeer hunters arrive ~10,500 BC at
Salaspils Laukskola on the Daugava [22]; the aurora stands over the moraines at dusk.
**2025**: the terrain draped in real Sentinel-2 imagery (EOX s2cloudless, CC-BY 4.0
[23]) with the simulated forest placed exactly where the satellite sees forest; the
quiet parish of ~347 people; the tower on the family hill.

The engine gained a physical sky driving the light, wind through every plant, a
walked-at-eye-level mode and free flight, near-field grass, terrain detail and water
reflections — camera feel, wind law and realism standards adapted from **LAAS**, an
MIT-licensed fully procedural WebGPU world [25].

Additional sources: [21] Stivriņš et al. 2017, *The Holocene* (dead-ice meltdown);
Zelčs et al., *Deglaciation history of Latvia*. [22] *Salaspils Laukskola Revisited*,
Archaeologia Baltica. [23] Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX (CC-BY 4.0, modified Copernicus
data). [24] EnterGauja — Brežģa kalns tower. [25] github.com/Braffolk/fable5-world-demo.
