## 1. GEOGRAPHY

### Location and Coordinates

Taurene village center sits at **57deg 9'34"N, 25deg 39'53"E**, elevation approximately 205 m above sea level [1]. The village lies on the banks of the Gauja river near Dabaru ezers, along highway P30 (Cesis--Vecpiebalga--Madona). It is 34 km from Cesis (the municipal center), 3.9 km from Dzerbene, 14.5 km from Vecpiebalga, and approximately 111 km from Riga [1][2].

Nekena muiza (the manor complex) stands at or adjacent to the village center -- the village effectively grew around the manor.

### Landform and Geomorphology

Taurenes pagasts (102.2 km2 total; 99.0 km2 land, 3.2 km2 water) sits within the **Vidzeme Upland** (Vidzemes augstiene), specifically at the junction of three sub-regions: the northwestern part is Mezole Hill region, the central-northeastern part falls within the Upper Gauja Depression (Augsgaujas ieplaka), and the southeastern part belongs to the Piebalga Hill region (Piebalgas pauguraina) [3][4].

The terrain is glacially sculpted -- subglacial channels with chains of through-flow lakes, moraine hills, and irregular plateaus. The Vidzeme Upland is characterized by large flat-topped elevations separated by subglacial channels containing lake chains [5]. Drumlins exist in the broader region but the Taurene area itself is best described as hilly moraine terrain with subglacial meltwater channel lakes, not a drumlin field proper [6].

**Brezga kalns** (also historically called Karatavu kalns -- "Gallows Hill") is the highest point in the parish at **255.4 m** above sea level, with 40 m relative height, approximately 1 km long and up to 450 m wide, forested at summit [7][8]. An 11-meter observation tower was opened there in 2017, offering views of Lake Alauksts, Lake Zobols, Inesi village, Elkas Hill, and the rolling Piebalga countryside [9]. From the tower the landscape reads as the quintessential Vidzeme mosaic: spruce-forested hilltops, fields on gentle slopes, farmstead clusters, and blue lake surfaces between moraines [5].

### Water: The Gauja and the Lake Chain

The **Gauja** -- Latvia's longest river (452 km) -- has its historical source at Lake Alauksts (202.3 m elevation, in neighboring Vecpiebalgas pagasts) [10]. The young river, still called Gaujina at its origin, flows northwest through a remarkable chain of seven interconnected subglacial through-flow lakes within Taurenes pagasts:

**Banuzu** > **Ilzes** > **Rijas** > **Brenkuzu** > **Stupenu** > **Dabaru** > **Taurenes** [3][4]

These are separate lakes, not one body with multiple names. Specifically:

- **Dabaru ezers** (also called Dabaru-Stupenu ezers or Stupenu ezers): 57deg 09'06"N, 25deg 39'09"E. Area 17.8 ha, max depth 5.5 m. The Gauja river flows through it. The western shore hosts the Brediqu (Lejstupu) hillfort. Taurene village sits on its eastern bank. [11][12]
- **Taurenes ezers** (also called Taurena ezers or Taurinu ezers): 57deg 09'41"N, 25deg 41'34"E. Area 31.6 ha, max depth 4.6 m. Located in the northern part of the parish, about 1.5 km ENE of the village center. The Gauja flows in from Kalenitis and exits toward Slicenis. Swimming beach on south shore. [13]

These are **two distinct lakes** -- Dabaru ezers is adjacent to Taurene village on the west/southwest, while Taurenes ezers is north-northeast of the center. Both are in the Gauja through-flow system.

**On "Brenkuzi" and the distillery connection:** The Slideshare parish presentation confirms that Nekena muiza operated a substantial **ale brewery** on the Gauja riverbank in the 19th century, producing 25,000 buckets annually, with ale transported via rails to arched stone cellars built into the riverbank for cooling [7]. The lake name "Brenkuzu ezers" is phonetically consistent with the German *Brennhaus* or *Branntweinhaus* (distillery/brewery building), suggesting the lake took its name from the manor's brewing operation on its shore. This is my inference, not a documented etymology, but the geographic coincidence and the confirmed brewery make it highly plausible.

### Nearby Lakes Beyond the Parish

- **Lake Alauksts**: 57deg 05'37"N, 25deg 46'12"E. Area 7.75 km2 (the largest lake in the immediate area), elevation 202.3 m. Source of the Gauja. Two islands. 9 fish species including pike, perch, bream. Approximately 10 km ESE of Taurene village. [10]
- **Araisi lake** (Araisu ezers): 57deg 15'07"N, 25deg 16'58"E, elevation 121 m. Site of the famous 9th-10th century Latgalian lake fortress. Approximately 7-10 km south of Cesis. Distance from Taurene to Araisi is roughly 27-30 km by road (via P30 to Cesis then south). [14]

### Forest Composition

Typical for the Vidzeme Upland at this elevation: **Norway spruce** (Picea abies) dominates the hillocks and slopes, with **Scots pine** (Pinus sylvestris) on drier sandy ridges, **birch** (Betula pendula and B. pubescens) in mixed stands and secondary growth, and **alder** (Alnus incana, A. glutinosa) in wetter lowlands and streamsides [5][15]. The Cultural Canon of Latvia specifically describes the Vidzeme hillock landscape as spruce-forested heights alternating with agricultural fields on gentler slopes, with farmsteads on hilltops [5].

## 2. NEKENA MUIZA / NOTKENSHOF

### Ownership History

**IMPORTANT:** There are *two* different "Nekenu muiza" entries in Latvian sources. The one relevant here is in **Taurenes pagasts, Cesu novads** (formerly Vecpiebalgas novads). A separate Nekunu muiza exists in Alsviku pagasts, Aluksnes novads -- that one is unrelated. [16]

The Taurene manor's documented history:

- **1426**: Riga Archbishop Hennings grants the estate (then called **Gerslau** or **Jorslava**) to his vassal Johan Frogt [3][17]
- **1431-1439**: Two manors documented in the territory -- "Tauren" (granted to Hans Rennem) and Jorslava manor (granted to Johannam Fogedam) [3]
- **~1601**: The estate passes to a family named **Netken/Notken** (German: Nötken), and is renamed Notkenshof / Nekena muiza after them [3][17]
- **1856**: Guards Lieutenant **Augusts Joachims von Panders** becomes owner [4][17]
- **1880s**: His successor **Heinrich von Pander** commissions the new manor house [17][18]
- **1905**: During the revolution, Nekena is one of few Vidzeme manors to survive unburned -- local peasants and servants actively protect it, attributed to the Pander family's humane management. (Neighboring Vecpiebalga, Sermuksi, Kaives, and Dzerbenes manors all burned.) [7][17]
- **1906**: A field court-martial is established in the new palace; cellars converted to prison [18][19]
- **1920-1922**: Latvian agrarian reform: Nekina muiza divided into 72 land units (1,150 ha total); Lodes muiza into 38 units (785 ha) [4]

The **von Pander** family (German: *von Pander*) were Baltic German nobility registered in the Vidzeme knighthood (Vidzemes brunieku matrikula). They owned the estate for approximately 66 years (1856-1922). Baroness **Anna von Pander** is specifically named as having protected an ancient burial site discovered during construction near Zalkalns [7].

### Architecture

The **new manor house** (Jauna pils) was built **1888-~1900** (roof-raising ceremony August 1888; construction complete by 1900) in **Neo-Renaissance** style inspired by Italian Renaissance villa architecture, designed by **Reinholds Georgs Smelings** (1840-1917), who was Riga's city architect from 1879-1915 [4][17][18][20]. Smelings studied in Italy and his early career was dominated by Neo-Renaissance commissions -- the Nekena villa is one of his rural works outside Riga [20].

An older manor house (18th century) also survives, built on the Gauja riverbank [7].

### The Ensemble Today

The manor center preserves **eight buildings** [17][19]:
1. New manor house (Jauna pils / kungu maja) -- now parish administration + culture center
2. Old manor house (Veca pils) -- 18th century, on Gauja bank
3. Barn (klets)
4. Ice house (ledus pagrabs) -- now houses Taurene Parish Museum
5. Brewery cellar (alus pagrabs) -- three arched stone cellars built into riverbank
6. Former horse-post building
7. Horse stable
8. Cattle shed

Additionally, approximately twenty farm buildings originally existed [17]. The **manor park** was rebuilt in the mid-19th century and still exists [17].

### Current Use

The new manor houses: Taurene parish administration (pagasta parvalde), a cultural center (kulturas nams), a library (biblioteka), and a local history museum (novadpetniecibas muzejs). A pharmacy and medical clinic also operate in the village [1]. The manor complex is a registered cultural monument but the main building is viewable only from the outside for casual visitors [17]. The local history exhibition features family relics, historical currency, biographies of notable locals, and Soviet-era kolkhoz documentation [17].

## 3. THE RENAMING: FROM NEKENE TO TAURENE

### The Facts

- The territory was called **"Tauren"** in 1431-1439 documents, then the name disappeared after the Thirty Years' War (which devastated the region) [3]
- From ~1601, the area was known as **Nekena/Nekina** (after the Notken/Netken family) [3]
- **1925**: At a parish council (pagasta valdes) meeting, teacher **Karlis Bormanis** proposed renaming the parish to restore the ancient name "Taurene," while retaining "Nekena" for the manor itself [3][4][21]
- **January 1, 1926**: The change takes official effect. Nekena pagasts becomes **Taurenes pagasts** [4][21]

### Karlis Bormanis (1877-1958)

Born February 22, 1877 at **Kalna Stimiku** farmstead in Nekena parish (now Taurene) -- or alternatively described as from "Zalkalns" (also called "Bormani") near the manor [7][22]. Education: Nekena and Dzerbene parish schools, then Valka-Valmiera Teachers' Seminary/Cander Institute in St. Petersburg (graduated 1897) [22].

Career: Taught at Riktere, Kempji, and Taurene parish schools. From 1923-1939, teacher and administrator at Taurene Primary School (subjects: local studies, history, geography, singing, German). Also directed choirs, staged theatrical productions, managed the local agricultural society, organized mazpulki (youth groups), edited the "Gaujas Calendar" (1920-1923), and contributed to the Mlenbach-Endzelln Latvian dictionary from 1894 onward [3][22].

His major published work: **"Zeme"** (Earth/Land) -- described as Latvia's **first geography textbook** for schools, first published 1908, later revised and republished [22]. He authored seven books total and is credited as the primary preserver of Taurene parish history [7][22].

Awarded the **Order of Three Stars, 5th class** [22]. Died May 5, 1958; buried in Dzerbene Old Cemetery [22].

### The Aurochs Story

Bormanis's reasoning, as documented in multiple sources: the ancient name "Tauren" derives from **taurs** (Latvian: aurochs, *Bos primigenius*). The parish history page states he argued the community should "not forget its own history" and restore the name [3][21]. The Latvijas Vestnesis 1998 article frames it as local intellectuals reasoning that "since the forests contained these animals, the parish should be named accordingly" [23].

The Slideshare parish presentation shows the **parish emblem** features **aurochs horns**, indicating this etymology is officially embraced [7]. Latvian Wikipedia confirms that place names containing "taur-" (Taurene, Taurupe, Taurkalne) constitute evidence that aurochs survived in Latvia's territory until approximately the 12th century [24].

### The Phrase "tauri ganijas" ("where the aurochs grazed")

I could not locate this exact phrase in period sources accessible via periodika.lv or elsewhere online. The Vecpiebalga municipal site gives the closest documented formulation: "Long ago aurochs grazed in forests here. They disappeared, only the word remained" [attributed to Bormanis's proposal, per local history summaries] [21]. The Latvijas Vestnesis article (1998) opens with lines attributed to poet **Mara Zalite**: "Where will you fly, my aurochs? -- To Taurene, to Taurene!" [23]. The Slideshare presentation confirms a Zalite poem is used in connection with the parish, playing on the taurene/Taurene noun/proper-name distinction and aurochs horn imagery [7].

### Verification Status

**Verified:** Bormanis proposed the renaming at a 1925 parish council meeting; the change took effect Jan 1, 1926; the etymology connects to "taurs" (aurochs). **Unverified via primary period source:** The exact phrasing "tauri ganijas" as a quote from Bormanis or from a poem he wrote. Bormanis was primarily a pedagogue and textbook author, not a published poet -- calling him a "schoolteacher-poet" appears to be local tradition rather than a documented literary identity. The poetic connection to Taurene is through Mara Zalite (b. 1952), writing decades later, not Bormanis himself.

## 4. BREZGI / BREZGIS

### The Place Name

**Brezgis** is confirmed as a populated place (apdzivota vieta / ciems) in Taurenes pagasts [3][25]. The official name in Latvian Wikipedia and the LGIA place-name context is **"Brezgis"**, with the alternative name **"Brezga krogs"** (Brezga tavern/inn) [25].

**Coordinates:** 57deg 06'43"N, 25deg 42'13"E [25]
**Location:** 6.4 km south of Taurene parish center, near road P30
**Population:** ~15 residents (2007) [25]
**Distances:** 39.8 km from Cesis, 120 km from Riga [25]

The Latvian Wikipedia article on Taurenes pagasts lists "Brezgis" among the largest settled places in the parish (alongside Taurene, Banuzi, Jeruzi, Krustakrogs, Lodesmuiza, etc.) [3].

**Administrative history:** In 1945, a separate Brezga ciema padome (village council) was established. In 1954, Brezga village was merged back into Taurene [25][3].

### Connection to Brezga kalns and the Manor

The hill **Brezga kalns** (255.4 m) is located near the Brezgis settlement. This is the same hill also called Karatavu kalns (Gallows Hill) [7][8]. The 1937 photo in zudusilatvija.lv shows a "Norade uz Brezga kalnu" (road sign to Brezga hill) [26].

Crucially, the Slideshare parish presentation documents that the **Nekena muiza brewery** sold its ale to **"Brezgi" and "Laci" taverns** in the 19th century [7]. This confirms that "Brezgi" was a functioning inn/tavern (krogs) tied to the manor's economic system. The place name "Brezga krogs" directly reflects this function.

### The Surname Brezgis

The surname appears in the PMLP (Latvian Citizenship and Migration Affairs) database and in the Latvian Surnames Dictionary at uzvardi.lv (based on 1935 census data, Vidzeme volume published 2022) [27]. I was unable to access the specific entry for "Brezgis" in the online database.

**Etymology hypothesis:** The root is likely **not** a direct variant of "berzs" (birch) despite superficial similarity. The phonetics are different: "berzs" has a long e and palatal rz, while "brezg-" has a short e and a g-cluster. More plausible: the surname derives from the place name (Brezgi/Brezga krogs), which itself may relate to an older toponym or personal name. Many Latvian surnames were assigned in the 1820s-1860s emancipation period and often derive from the farmstead where a family lived. A family living at or near "Brezgi" would naturally take the surname "Brezgis."

**Spelling variants to note:** The place is "Brezgis" (with z and g), the hill is "Brezga kalns" -- all with the z-g cluster. This is distinct from "Brezgi" (without the palatalization mark) which appears in the brewery-tavern reference. These may be orthographic variants of the same name across different time periods and transcription conventions.

### What I Could Not Find

- No entry for "Brezgi" or "Brezgis" appeared when searching the LGIA vietvardi database directly (the 404 response suggests either the ID has changed or the specific entry requires different search parameters) [28]
- No old map showing "Brezgi" as a labeled farmstead was accessible online
- No documented etymology of the place name "Brezgi" in academic toponomy sources

### Registered Monuments in Taurenes Pagasts

That is **13 registered archaeological monuments** in one parish -- a remarkably dense concentration. Five are state-significance, eight are local-significance [29].

### Key Sites Described

**Banuzu pilskalns (ID 580):** Located 8 km southwest of Dzerbene, on an elevated piece of land 12 m high, bordered on the east by lake meadows and on the south by the Melnupe stream ravine. Fortified by two ditches (inner ~1m deep, outer ~20cm deep) with small embankments. Classified as a "very weak and crudely constructed fortification" -- likely a refuge for inhabitants and livestock during raids rather than a permanent military installation [30]. Now overgrown with forest [3]. Twelve springs flow from the hillside [7].

**Lejstupu pilskalns (ID 585):** Also called "Brediqu pilskalns." Located on the western shore of Dabaru ezers [12], directly adjacent to Taurene village. State-protected.

**Nekina senkapi (ID 581):** Ancient (pre-medieval) burial ground, state-protected. Location details not fully described in accessible sources but likely near the manor grounds, consistent with the "ancient burial ground discovered during New Manor construction" that Baroness Anna von Pander protected [7].

**Libiesu apmetne (ID 589) + Lejas Libiesu senkapi (ID 588):** A Livonian (Finno-Ugric) settlement and burial ground. The name "Libiesu" directly indicates ethnic Livonians (Livi/Libiesi). This is archaeologically significant -- it documents a Finno-Ugric population this far inland from the coast, in what is otherwise considered Latgalian territory [3][29].

**Zalkalnu senkapi (ID 592):** Located at Zalkalns, where Bormanis was born. The Slideshare presentation notes that a bronze arm brooch was found during construction near the new manor and is now in a museum [7].

**Banuzu Svetavots (ID 582):** A sacred spring in a low meadow near the Banuzu-Skujenes road. The spring is believed to date from the era when a wooden castle stood on the nearby hilltop. The water is reportedly pure, non-freezing, and associated with healing properties [7][30].

### The Velnakmens and Pekakmens

**Banuzu Velnakmens** (Devil's Stone / Vella Vartu akmens): A massive glacial erratic on the NW shore of Banuzu Lake. Dimensions: 2.22 m high, 4.4 m long, 4.5 m wide at base, 3 m at top, **15 m circumference**. Smooth surface with a deep crevice (1.2 m wide, 0.27 m deep) [7][31]. Legend: the Devil tried to build a bridge across Banuzu Lake, claiming every tenth person; Perkons (thunder god) kicked the bridge apart, scattering the stones [31].

**Pekakmens:** Referenced in the Latvian Wikipedia article on Taurenes pagasts as a "ritual site near Peku homestead" with the Velnakmens having circumference 15.5 m [3]. Limited additional detail found.

### Adjacent Parishes

**Dzerbene:** The medieval Dzerbene castle (Schloss Serben) was built on a Latgalian hillfort called **Augstais kalns** -- a dramatic formation with 20 m steep slopes, a 50x50 m summit platform, ramparts and two ditches. A 1786 drawing by J.C. Broce depicts it. The Christian practice of building churches atop ancient cult sites is documented here [32].

**Vecpiebalga:** Vecpiebalga Castle (Piebalgas pilsdrupas) -- a bishop's castle first documented 1224, built before 1318. Square plan ~57x49 m, walls survive to 3-6 m height. Fiefed to the Tisenhausen family 1361-1482. Destroyed by Muscovite troops in 1559 and 1577 [33].

### Iron Age Peoples

The area around Taurene was inhabited by **Latgalians** (Eastern Balts) from at least the 5th-6th centuries onward [34]. By the 9th-12th centuries, the region fell within or near the territory of **Talava**, one of the principal Latgalian polities (alongside Jersika, Koknese, Atzele, and Imera) [34][35]. The Araisi lake fortress (9th-10th c., ~30 km away) is the most spectacular surviving Latgalian site in the region [14].

However, the **"Libiesu apmetne"** (ID 589) and **"Lejas Libiesu senkapi"** (ID 588) document a **Livonian (Finno-Ugric)** presence in Taurene as well. Livonians (Livi) are typically associated with coastal areas and the lower Gauja/Daugava, but their presence this far inland suggests either mixed settlement or seasonal use. The Cesis district broadly shows over 40 burial fields from the 7th-12th centuries including both Baltic Finn barrows and Latgalian flat/barrow burials [34].

## 6. COLOR: LEGENDS, NOTABLE PEOPLE, VIVID DETAIL

### The White Lady (Balta Dama)

Nekena muiza has its own ghost -- a "white lady" who wanders the manor buildings on full-moon nights. According to local tradition, she was a servant girl who fell in love with the manor owner's son; the baron disapproved and had her immured (walled in). She reportedly enjoys dancing and has been seen by the local musician Andris Vilders when returning late from parties, and by the previous director of the Taurene culture center [36][37].

### Notable People from Taurene

- **Ernests Felsbergs** (1866-1928): Born at the forester's homestead "Nekenes Pikani." Art historian and first acting rector of the University of Latvia (1920-1922). Received initial education at Taurene and Dzerbene parish schools [38]. One of the most distinguished figures from this small parish.

- **Janis Alksnis** (1869-1939): Born in Taurene parish. Self-taught architect who designed over **130 buildings** in Riga between 1901-1914, most in Art Nouveau style. One of the greatest masters of "Perpendicular Art Nouveau" -- a distinctive formal trend of Riga's Art Nouveau architecture [39].

- **Mikelis Hans** (1810-1893): Self-taught physician and mental illness healer who lived at Baltini homestead (purchased from Nekena Manor in 1873 for 19 thaler 73 groschen). Used homeopathic remedies and cold water therapy. Documented in Professor H. Budulis's "Psychiatry" regarding 19th-century Latvian treatment methods. A pool at Baltini was used for hydrotherapy; patients with mental illness were reportedly tied to trees nearby. Buried at Dzerbene Old Cemetery [7].

- **Karlis Bormanis** (1877-1958): The schoolteacher who renamed the parish. Author of Latvia's first geography textbook. See Section 3.

### The 1905 Revolution and the Protected Manor

While manors burned across Vidzeme in 1905 (Vecpiebalga, Sermuksi, Kaives, and Dzerbenes all destroyed), Nekena survived because local peasants and servants physically confronted the arsonists [7][17]. This is attributed to the Pander family's unusually humane management -- a detail that survives in local memory as a point of pride. The irony: in 1906, the tsarist state used the spared building as a field court-martial location and converted its cellars into prison cells [18][19].

### The Brewery Economy

The 19th-century manor brewery on the Gauja riverbank produced 25,000 buckets of ale annually, distributed to taverns at Brezgi and Laci [7]. Three arched stone cellars were built into the riverbank for temperature-controlled storage, with ale transported by rail from the brewery to the cellars. A whey pipeline crossed the Gauja on wooden pipes to reach the pig facility. A documented 1898 incident: when a batch of ale spoiled, the supervisor received 21 lashes [7]. The brewery was destroyed in 1944 [7].

### The Lodes (Apsu) Lutheran Church

Built 1780 in log construction, preserving medieval wooden chapel form. Originally thatched (replaced with shingles early 20th century). Small diamond-patterned 18th-century windows survive. Altar, pulpit, and organ (6 registers, by M. Kreslins of Jekabpils, 1890) date to 1893. Bell cast 1893 in Bohuma, Germany. Tower added 1911. State-level architectural monument. Film "Aija" shot scenes here [7][40].

### The Banuzu Swedish Cemetery

A 19th-century cemetery near Velnakmens, established 1826 by the Banuzu Manor owner and the Skujenes baron (both Swedish). Stone gates inscribed "1826." The Porth family (baron's family: 3 sons, 6 daughters of 9 children) are buried there [7].

### Landscape as Experience

From Brezga kalns during Latvia's first independence period (1918-1940), theatrical performances were staged with the Piebalga landscape as backdrop -- a natural amphitheater. In 1937, A. Alunana's work was performed there; in 1939, G. Merkela's "Vanemu Imantu" [7]. The hill forces the Gauja to make a large loop toward Taurene [7].

The Cultural Canon of Latvia describes the experience of viewing Vidzeme hills: "in summer heat the forests are smoky blue, but in a rainy day under grey clouds -- almost completely black" [5]. The observation tower on Brezga kalns offers sunrise views of mist-covered meadows between the moraines [9].

## EDITORIAL NOTES

**What surprised me:** The density of archaeological sites (13 in one parish). The Livonian settlement (Libiesu apmetne, ID 589) this far from the coast. The manor brewery selling 25,000 buckets annually to the Brezgi tavern -- which directly connects Anna's family name to the manor's economic geography. The 1905 detail where peasants protect rather than burn their manor, making Nekena nearly unique in Vidzeme. And Ernests Felsbergs -- the first rector of Latvia's national university grew up in this tiny parish.

**For the 3D model:** The key terrain feature is the Gauja flowing through the subglacial lake chain, with Brezga kalns (255m) forcing the river into a large meander loop near Taurene village. The manor sits on the riverbank with its brewery cellars carved into the slope. Dabaru ezers lies west of the village; Taurenes ezers is north-northeast. The landscape is rolling moraines with spruce on the heights, birch/alder in the wet valleys, fields on the gentle slopes, and compact farmstead clusters on hilltops.

**Confidence levels:** Geography and manor facts are well-sourced. The renaming story (Bormanis, 1925, aurochs) is documented across multiple independent sources. The specific phrasing "tauri ganijas" as a Bormanis quote is local tradition -- I could not verify it in a period text. The Brezgi-to-Brezgis surname connection is my inference from circumstantial evidence (geographic coincidence + brewery-tavern relationship), not a documented etymology.
